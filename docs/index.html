The 100 Annals definitions in the Tau Ceti roadmaps

A list of 100 definitions has been drawn up as what is needed to state the main theorems of recent papers in the Annals of Mathematics. This page says, for each of them, which Tau Ceti roadmap specifies it and where in that roadmap to look. Tau Ceti is a Lean 4 library built on Mathlib; its work is planned in roadmaps, each one a folder holding the mathematical specification of a theory, written in layers.

The first view goes through the definitions by area. The second lists every roadmap that appears, with a short description and the definitions it covers. Layer and section names are those of the roadmap texts on the date below.

A definition is listed as already formalised only when every part of it has been checked in the source of Tau Ceti or Mathlib; at present none is. Where part of a definition is in the library, a note under it says exactly which part and names the files it was checked in. Such notes are shown only after that check.

Data read on 20 November 2026, 11:17 UTC. 100 definitions, 114 roadmaps. Of the statuses below, 6 hold only in part.

In the library or Mathlib: already formalised in Tau Ceti or in Mathlib; On main: specified in a roadmap that is on the main branch; Open pull request: specified in a roadmap that is in an open pull request; Planned: planned on the job board, with no roadmap text yet; Not yet planned: no roadmap specifies it.

By definition

Each definition lists the roadmaps to look at. Defines: the roadmap states the notion as something to construct. Defines in part: it does so for a special case or for one component. Uses: it builds on the notion. Related: it holds an ingredient or a neighbouring notion, and neither defines nor uses this one. A status marked in part means that no roadmap yet defines the notion in full; the note under the definition says what is missing.

Real rank of a semisimple Lie group

On main

The real rank of a real semisimple Lie algebra is the dimension of a maximal abelian subspace of the −1 eigenspace of a Cartan involution, which does not depend on the choices; the real rank of a Lie group with semisimple Lie algebra is that of its Lie algebra.

Where to look first. RealSemisimpleLieGroups states the definition, for Lie algebras and for groups. RepresentationTheory/LieGroups holds the Cartan and Iwasawa decompositions it rests on, and LieGroupsPartII extends the conjugacy of maximal abelian subspaces to groups that need not be connected or semisimple.

  • Related RepresentationTheory/LieGroups (on main; about this roadmap)Layer 9: the Cartan, Iwasawa and KAK decompositionsholds the Iwasawa decomposition, with its maximal abelian subspace and restricted roots, on which the definition rests; the real rank itself is not named there
  • Related LieGroupsPartII (on main; about this roadmap)Layer 2, section 2.2: conjugacy of maximal abelian subspaces of the −1 eigenspace of a Cartan involutionextends the conjugacy statement to groups that need not be connected or semisimple

Tau Ceti has the Cartan decomposition G = K·exp(𝔭) of the general and special linear groups over ℝ and ℂ and of the real symplectic group, and the Iwasawa decomposition G = KAN of the general linear group over ℝ and ℂ and of SL(n, ℝ). It has no Cartan involution of a general real semisimple Lie algebra, no restricted roots and no real rank. Checked in: Cartan decomposition of general and special linear groups; Cartan decomposition of the real symplectic group; Iwasawa decomposition of the general linear group; Iwasawa decomposition of SL(n, ℝ).

Parabolic subgroups of a real semisimple Lie group (minimal and standard parabolics)

On main

In a connected real semisimple Lie group G with finite centre and Iwasawa decomposition G = KAN, the minimal parabolic subgroup is P = MAN with M the centraliser of A in K; the standard parabolic subgroups are the subgroups containing P, indexed by the sets of simple restricted roots, and a parabolic subgroup is a conjugate of one of them; each has a Langlands decomposition.

Where to look first. RealSemisimpleLieGroups states the whole definition, for connected groups with semisimple Lie algebra and finite centre. The minimal parabolic subgroup alone is also stated in AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups, for the real points of reductive groups, and in LieGroupsPartII, for reductive groups that need not be connected.

What Tau Ceti has. Tau Ceti has parabolic subgroups of an affine algebraic group over a field, defined as the smooth closed subgroups that contain a Borel subgroup over an algebraic closure; the subgroup attached to a cocharacter of an affine group scheme, with its Levi decomposition on points; and the standard parabolic subgroups of a group with a Tits system. Infinitesimal character (and Casimir eigenvalue) of a representation of a real reductive group

On main

A module over the enveloping algebra of the complexified Lie algebra of a real reductive group has an infinitesimal character if the centre of the enveloping algebra acts on it by scalars; by the Harish-Chandra isomorphism the resulting character of the centre is a Weyl group orbit of linear forms on a Cartan subalgebra, and the scalar by which the Casimir element acts is the Casimir eigenvalue.

Where to look first. AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups states the definition for representations of real reductive groups; it relies on RepresentationTheory/LieHighestWeight for the Harish-Chandra isomorphism of the Lie algebra.

  • Defines AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups (on main; about this roadmap)Layer 2, section 2.2: infinitesimal characters and Harish-Chandra finitenesssmooth vectors of Banach representations are section 2.7, and the Casimir values for GL₂(ℝ) are in section 2.6
  • Defines in part RepresentationTheory/LieHighestWeight (on main; about this roadmap)Layer 7 (the centre of the enveloping algebra and the Harish-Chandra isomorphism); Layer 9, section 9.2 (reductive enveloping centres)the Lie algebra part only: the Harish-Chandra isomorphism for semisimple and reductive Lie algebras; representations of groups are not treated

What Tau Ceti has. Tau Ceti has, for a finite-dimensional Lie algebra with nondegenerate Killing form over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, the Harish-Chandra isomorphism from the centre of the enveloping algebra onto the Weyl group invariants, and the theorem that two weights give the same central character exactly when they lie in one orbit of the shifted Weyl group action. It also has the Casimir element, proved central, with the scalar by which it acts on a highest weight module, and the modules over an algebra on which the centre acts through a given character. The infinitesimal character of a representation of a real reductive group, taken on its smooth vectors, is not there. Checked in: the Harish-Chandra isomorphism; the Casimir element is central; the Casimir scalar on highest weight modules; modules with a given central character.

Convergence of a spectral sequence

On main

A spectral sequence converges to a family of filtered objects Hⁿ when its limit page is identified with the graded pieces of the filtrations and each filtration is exhaustive and separated; weak convergence drops the two conditions on the filtrations, and strong convergence adds completeness.

Where to look first. SpectralSequencesAndConvergence states the definition; SpectralSequencesAndConvergencePartII constructs the standard spectral sequences and proves that they converge in this sense.

  • Defines SpectralSequencesAndConvergence (on main; about this roadmap)Layer 1, section 1.4 (the limit page); Layer 3, section 3.2 (convergence structures)finite filtrations are section 3.4 and convergence in a range of degrees section 4.4

What Tau Ceti has. Tau Ceti has, for a spectral object in an abelian category, the abutment with its filtration and the identification of the limit terms with the graded pieces. Convergence, weak or strong, is not defined as a notion for an arbitrary spectral sequence. Checked in: abutment of a spectral object and its filtration; convergence for a finite filtration; convergence for exhaustive filtrations; convergence for complete filtrations.

Split classical groups Sp2n, SOl and general spin groups GSpinl

On main, in part

Over a field of characteristic not 2, Sp2n is the group preserving the standard alternating form, SOl is the special orthogonal group of the split quadratic form in l variables, and GSpinl is the split reductive group with a prescribed root datum, a central extension of SOl by the multiplicative group that can be realised in the even Clifford algebra.

Where to look first. AutomorphicLFunctionsAndLocalFactorsPartII constructs the general spin group and fixes the three families; EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison uses the groups of small rank.

Note. Only the general spin group is specified, in AutomorphicLFunctionsAndLocalFactorsPartII, through its Clifford model over a field of characteristic zero and without the description by a root datum. The symplectic group is already in Tau Ceti, and the special orthogonal group of the split form is not singled out in any roadmap.

  • Uses EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison (on main; about this roadmap)Layer 11 (local unitary packets and global classification), sections 11.0f, 11.0h and 11.1uses Sp₄, GSpin₄ and GSpin₅ in the transfers of rank four

The special orthogonal group of the split form is not singled out among these, and there are no general spin groups. The Hodge structure on H1 of a complex abelian variety

On main

For a complex abelian variety A, the group H¹(A(ℂ), ℚ) carries a pure Hodge structure of weight one, whose (1,0) part is the space of holomorphic differentials, and every ample line bundle on A polarizes it.

Where to look first. AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli states it, in its layer on complex uniformization. ShimuraData builds on it to define Hodge-generic abelian varieties.

Checked in: weight-one Hodge structure of a lattice; Riemann forms are the polarizations.

The volume form ωn and integration on a compact Kähler (or Hermitian) manifold

On main

On a complex manifold of dimension n with a Hermitian form ω, the top power ωⁿ is a positive volume form; it gives a measure, integrals of functions and, on a compact manifold, the volume, which for a Kähler form depends only on the cohomology class of ω.

Where to look first. ComplexKahlerGeometry states it: Hermitian metrics with their fundamental forms, Kähler metrics, and the identity between the Riemannian volume form of a Hermitian metric and ωⁿ/n!. ComplexHessianEquations adds the integration identities, including that the integrals do not change when a Kähler form moves in its class.

  • Defines ComplexKahlerGeometry (on main; about this roadmap)Conventions (a Hermitian metric and its fundamental form); Layer 5, section 5.9 (the Riemannian volume form is ωⁿ/n! for the complex orientation); Layer 6, section 6.1 (Kähler metrics)Hermitian metrics need not be Kähler here; integration is against the Riemannian volume, which this identity compares with ωⁿ
  • Uses ComplexHessianEquations (on main; about this roadmap)Layer 3, section 3.2: form identities and integration identitiescompares ωⁿ with the Riemannian volume and proves that the integrals are unchanged when a Kähler form moves in its class

What Tau Ceti has. Formalised in part. Tau Ceti has, for any smooth two-form ω on a manifold of real dimension 2n, closed or not, the form ωⁿ/n! and the measure it defines, which is locally finite and so finite on a compact manifold. A Hermitian form on a complex manifold is expressed there as a two-form compatible with the complex structure of the manifold, without a name of its own, and the Kähler condition adds that the form is closed. The comparison of this measure with the Riemannian volume of the associated metric, and the invariance of the total volume in a Kähler class, are not there. Checked in: the form ωⁿ/n! of a two-form; the measure of that volume form; the Kähler condition; compatibility of a two-form with a complex structure.
